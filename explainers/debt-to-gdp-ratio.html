Debt to GDP ratio: what it means and how it is calculated

Explainer · Fiscal policy · Educational only · Updated 12 Nov 2026

The debt to GDP ratio is a government's debt divided by the country's gross domestic product, written as a percentage. A ratio of 100% means the government owes as much as the whole economy produces in a year. It is the standard way to compare debt across countries and decades, because a raw dollar figure says little without the size of the economy beside it.

This page is for AP Macroeconomics and intro economics study. It is educational only and is not investment, financial, tax, or legal advice. It makes no forecast about any economy.

The formula

Debt to GDP = government debt ÷ nominal GDP × 100

Both parts are in money terms at current prices. FRED's series GFDEGDQ188S spells out its own version: total public debt (series GFDEBTN, converted from millions to billions of dollars) divided by nominal GDP, times 100. Debt is a stock, measured at a point in time. GDP is a flow, measured over a period. The ratio mixes the two, which is fine as long as you remember that the deficit, not the debt, is the yearly flow. The deficit vs debt explainer covers that distinction.

A hypothetical worked example

A country owes $3 trillion and its nominal GDP is $2.5 trillion. Its ratio is 3 ÷ 2.5 × 100 = 120%. Next year it borrows another $0.1 trillion, so debt is $3.1 trillion, while nominal GDP grows 6% to $2.65 trillion. The ratio becomes 3.1 ÷ 2.65 × 100 ≈ 117%. The debt went up, but the ratio went down, because the economy grew faster than the debt. These numbers are invented for teaching.

What moves the ratio

  • Deficits. Each year's deficit adds to the debt and pushes the ratio up.
  • Nominal GDP growth. Real growth and inflation both raise the denominator and push the ratio down. See nominal vs real GDP.
  • Interest rates. Interest on existing debt adds to future deficits. When the interest rate is below the growth rate of nominal GDP, a country can run small deficits and still see the ratio stabilise.

The latest US figure

FRED reports US total public debt at about 121.2% of GDP in the second quarter of 2026, against about 122.4% in the first quarter and about 118.2% in the second quarter of 2025, retrieved 12 November 2026. This measure counts total public debt, which includes debt held by government trust funds. Measures of debt held by the public are lower, so always check which one a headline uses.

Common exam mistakes

  • Saying the ratio fell because the government paid off debt. Usually it falls because GDP grew faster.
  • Comparing a gross debt ratio for one country with a net or held-by-the-public ratio for another.
  • Using real GDP as the denominator. Debt is in current dollars, so the ratio uses nominal GDP.

Frequently asked questions

What is the debt to GDP ratio?

It is government debt divided by gross domestic product, shown as a percentage. It compares the stock of debt with the size of the economy that supports it.

How is the US debt to GDP ratio calculated on FRED?

FRED series GFDEGDQ188S divides total public debt by nominal GDP, converting debt from millions to billions of dollars first, and multiplies by 100.

What is the latest US debt to GDP ratio?

FRED reports total public debt at about 121.2% of GDP in the second quarter of 2026, retrieved 12 November 2026.

Can debt to GDP fall while debt rises?

Yes. If nominal GDP grows faster than the debt, the ratio falls even though the amount owed goes up.
