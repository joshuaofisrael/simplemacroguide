U-3 vs U-6 unemployment: the official rate and the broader measure

Explainer · Labor · Educational only · Updated 12 Nov 2026

The headline US unemployment rate is only one official measure of unused labor. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes a set of labor underutilization rates. U-3 is the official unemployment rate you see in headlines. U-6 is a broader rate that also counts people who are marginally attached to the labor force and people who are working part time because they cannot find full-time work.

This page is for AP Macroeconomics and intro economics study. It is educational only and is not investment, financial, tax, or legal advice. It makes no forecast about any economy.

What U-3 counts

FRED series UNRATE is the official unemployment rate from the Current Population Survey. The notes say it is the number of people aged 16 and over who are actively searching for a job, as a percentage of the labor force, and that this rate is also defined as the U-3 measure of labor underutilization. To be unemployed in this sense you must be jobless, available for work and actively looking. People who have stopped looking are out of the labor force, not unemployed. That is the same distinction drawn in the participation rate explainer.

U-3 = unemployed ÷ labor force × 100

What U-6 adds

FRED series U6RATE is titled, in full, "Total Unemployed, Plus All Persons Marginally Attached to the Labor Force, Plus Total Employed Part Time for Economic Reasons, as a Percent of the Civilian Labor Force Plus All Persons Marginally Attached to the Labor Force (U-6)." In plainer words:

  • Unemployed is the same U-3 group.
  • Marginally attached people want a job, have looked in the recent past, but did not look in the last four weeks, so they are not in the labor force this month. Discouraged workers are a subset of this group.
  • Part time for economic reasons means people who are already employed, but working shorter hours because they cannot find full-time work or because their hours were cut.

U-6 = (unemployed + marginally attached + part time for economic reasons) ÷ (labor force + marginally attached) × 100

Notice the denominator. Marginally attached people are added to both the top and the bottom. People who are part time for economic reasons are already in the labor force, so they are already in the denominator; U-6 only adds them to the numerator.

A hypothetical worked example

A labor force of 100 million includes 4 million unemployed and 3 million who are part time for economic reasons. Another 2 million are marginally attached. U-3 is 4 ÷ 100 × 100 = 4%. U-6 is (4 + 2 + 3) ÷ (100 + 2) × 100 ≈ 8.8%. These numbers are invented for teaching.

The latest US figures

For October 2026, FRED reports U-3 at 4.1% and U-6 at 7.9%, both seasonally adjusted, retrieved 12 November 2026. A month earlier, in September 2026, U-3 was 4.2% and U-6 was 7.6%. The gap itself moves: U-6 can rise even when U-3 is flat if more people are stuck in part-time work or on the edge of the labor force. Neither series splits unemployment into the frictional, structural and cyclical types covered in the types of unemployment explainer.

Common exam mistakes

  • Putting part-time-for-economic-reasons workers in the U-3 numerator. They are employed, so they are not in official unemployment.
  • Forgetting to add the marginally attached to the U-6 denominator as well as the numerator.
  • Calling U-6 the "real" unemployment rate and U-3 a fake one. Both are official BLS measures; they answer different questions.

Frequently asked questions

What is U-3 unemployment?

U-3 is the official unemployment rate: unemployed people as a percentage of the labor force. FRED's notes on UNRATE say this is also defined as the U-3 measure of labor underutilization.

What is U-6 unemployment?

FRED defines U-6 as total unemployed, plus all persons marginally attached to the labor force, plus total employed part time for economic reasons, as a percent of the civilian labor force plus all persons marginally attached to the labor force.

Why is U-6 always higher than U-3?

U-6 starts with the same unemployed people as U-3 and then adds two extra groups to the numerator, and it also widens the denominator by the marginally attached. The added groups cannot be negative, so U-6 sits above U-3.

What are the latest US U-3 and U-6 rates?

In October 2026, FRED reports U-3 (UNRATE) at 4.1% and U-6 (U6RATE) at 7.9%, both seasonally adjusted, retrieved 12 November 2026.
